# HCOS™ CME Alignment

## Activity

**The Future of AI May Feel Like One Relationship**

### Educational positioning

HCOS™ Insight + Architectural Forecast

### Audience

Healthcare professionals involved in clinical care, pharmacy, informatics, operations, access, quality, safety, governance, leadership, and AI-enabled workflow design.

---

## Educational Purpose

This activity is designed to help healthcare professionals recognize that a simple AI experience may depend on complex activity occurring beneath the interface.

The activity does not require learners to become AI engineers.

Instead, it develops enough architectural awareness for healthcare professionals to ask meaningful questions about:

- identity;
- context;
- information access;
- memory;
- delegated authority;
- human review;
- interoperability;
- reliability;
- and accountability.

## Central Educational Problem

Healthcare organizations may begin placing increasingly capable AI on top of information systems that were largely designed for human navigation rather than continuous AI coordination.

The educational concern is therefore not limited to whether an AI model is accurate.

It is whether healthcare organizations can understand, govern, explain, correct, and remain accountable for the activity occurring beneath a smooth AI interface.

> **The interface may look calm. The work beneath it may not be.**

---

## Suggested Learning Progression

### 1. Introduce HCOS™

Explain HCOS™ as a human-centered way of evaluating technology, workflow, policy, governance, and organizational design.

### 2. Introduce the Forecast

People may increasingly state goals rather than manually choose applications.

### 3. Show the Hidden Architecture

One request may involve many models, tools, systems, organizations, and permissions.

### 4. Translate to Healthcare

Use a healthcare example such as helping a patient start a complex therapy.

### 5. Introduce the Undercurrent

The healthcare professional may see one result without seeing every system, data source, handoff, permission decision, or delegated action underneath it.

### 6. Explain the Infrastructure Gap

Current EHR environments commonly rely on human users to provide context, interpret conflicting information, reconcile records, recognize exceptions, and remain responsible.

### 7. Separate Assistance from Action

Use the **AI Action and Authority Continuum** to show that summarizing or drafting is different from sending, submitting, modifying, ordering, or completing.

### 8. Provide a Practical Review Method

Ask:

- Who is acting?
- Which context is active?
- What information is necessary?
- What may persist?
- What authority is delegated?
- What requires human review?
- Who remains accountable?

### 9. Apply to Organizational Readiness

Healthcare organizations should evaluate whether the surrounding architecture can safely support deeper AI coordination.

### 10. Close with Stewardship

The goal is not to avoid AI.

The goal is to enter deliberately, begin with bounded uses, preserve visible accountability, and avoid allowing invisible coordination to advance faster than organizational understanding and governance.

---

## Suggested Learning Objectives

After participating in this activity, learners should be able to:

1. Differentiate a continuous AI experience from the distributed systems and workflows operating underneath it.
2. Describe why identity, context, memory, delegated authority, and accountability are relevant to healthcare AI coordination.
3. Identify infrastructure and workflow risks that may become more difficult to see as AI performs work across multiple systems.
4. Apply a practical stewardship lens to determine whether an AI-enabled healthcare workflow is ready for deeper coordination.

## Suggested Knowledge Checks

### Knowledge Check 1 — Hidden Architecture

A healthcare professional asks an AI assistant to help a patient start a specialty medication. The assistant reviews the EHR, payer requirements, pharmacy information, and follow-up scheduling.

**What is the most important architectural concern?**

A. Whether all tasks are performed by the same AI model  
B. Whether the interface looks simple to the healthcare professional  
C. Whether each system operates within appropriate identity, information, authority, and review boundaries  
D. Whether the AI can complete the workflow without human involvement

**Correct answer: C**

### Knowledge Check 2 — Assistance vs. Action

An AI drafts a patient message and a prior authorization request. It then sends both automatically before professional review.

**What changed in the governance state?**

A. Nothing; drafting and sending are equivalent  
B. The AI moved from preparing work to performing delegated action  
C. The AI became more accurate  
D. The workflow became fully interoperable

**Correct answer: B**

### Knowledge Check 3 — Infrastructure Readiness

A health system wants AI to coordinate work across the EHR, specialty pharmacy, payer portal, and patient messaging system.

**What should the organization do first?**

A. Give the AI broad access so it can determine the most efficient process  
B. Automate the existing workflow before redesigning it  
C. Map the workflow, information boundaries, authority levels, review points, and accountability  
D. Select the largest available AI model

**Correct answer: C**

### Knowledge Check 4 — The Undercurrent

A healthcare team receives an AI-generated outcome but cannot determine which data sources were used or whether an external system changed the workflow state.

**What does this illustrate?**

A. A user-interface problem only  
B. The need for greater model creativity  
C. An undercurrent that the organization cannot adequately explain or govern  
D. Evidence that AI should never be used in healthcare

**Correct answer: C**

---

## Final CME Takeaway

> **Healthcare should not ask only whether AI can complete the work. It must ask whether the surrounding system can safely support, explain, govern, and remain accountable for how the work is completed.**

The goal is not to stay out of the water.

The goal is to understand the current, enter deliberately, and preserve human-centered boundaries as AI becomes more capable.
